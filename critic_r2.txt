--- BLOCKER -------------------------------------------------------
None confirmed. The four MAJORs below are all reproducible and two of them cost the
player most of a fight, but none of them is a hard lock of the sequencer itself.

--- MAJOR 1: WEAPON ON does not survive a hat change. A spinner was commanded OFF
    for 91% of a real fight by a program that never contains WEAPON OFF.

PROGRAM (battery E, run as a real L1C1 career AUTONOMY fight vs SCOUT, playerSource=Program):
  h0 FLIPPED  WHEN Flipped > 0.5      [ TURN 100% 1.0s ]
  h1 WALL     WHEN EdgeDist < 1.5     [ MOVE AWAY WALL 85% 1.0s ; TURN BY 90 ]
  h2 HURT     WHEN HpFrac < 0.5       [ MOVE AWAY ENEMY 70% 1.5s ]
  h3 STRIKE   WHEN EnemyRange < 2.0   [ WEAPON ON ; MOVE TO ENEMY 100% 1.0s ]
  h4 SEEK     WHEN always             [ WEAPON ON ; MOVE TO ENEMY 70% 1.5s ]
  Validate() = OK. AutonomyBlocker = allowed.

WHAT THE PROGRAM SAYS: the weapon is switched ON and never switched off. Two hats turn
it on; nothing anywhere turns it off.

MEASURED (26.0 s sampled at the physics rate, actuator.aiFire integrated):
  aiFire true for  9%  of the fight  ( = 2.42 s )
  hat dwell:  FLIP 20.82 s   WALL 2.76 s   HURT 0.00 s   STRIKE 1.86 s   SEEK 0.56 s
  STRIKE dwell + SEEK dwell = 2.42 s -- EXACTLY the aiFire time, to the sample.
  weapon commanded OFF for the whole 2.76 s the WALL hat was in charge.

CONTROL LEG (battery F): same rig, same opponent, same four hats, one change - every hat
now begins with WEAPON ON.
  aiFire = 99.8% of 24.0 s. aiFire==false on 2 physics steps out of 1200.
  => the 9% in E was caused by the missing repeat of WEAPON ON, nothing else.

DIAGNOSIS: ProgramRunner.StartHat() and ReleaseHat() both call ZeroChannels(), which does
  for (i) { actFire[i] = false; actSign[i] = 1f; }
Every handover therefore kills the weapon, and only a hat whose own body contains a
POp.Weapon block turns it back on. The V2 channel rule ("ANY handover zeroes everything")
is deliberate for WHEELS -- motors should coast on a handover. Applying it to the weapon
latch means there is NO WAY to express "keep the blade spinning" in the language: the
player must paste WEAPON ON into the top of every hat including safety hats, and the
canvas gives no hint of it. A wall touch or a flip silently disarms the machine.

--- MAJOR 2: MOVE AWAY FROM WALL, held as a standing order, never converges. It flaps
    across the arena at full power and spends ~half of every cycle REDUCING the wall
    distance it is supposed to increase.

PROGRAM (battery D3), wall aimed dead ahead at the start:
  h0 WHEN always [ MOVE AWAY WALL 80% (SET, no seconds/rounds) ; WAIT 3.0 ]

WHAT THE PROGRAM SAYS: increase the distance to the wall and hold that.

MEASURED (wallDist / wallBearing / mean wheel command, 0.25 s samples):
  t=0.00 d2.76 b   0 c-0.80      t=2.50 d4.60 b 180 c+0.80
  t=0.75 d4.19 b   0 c-0.80      t=3.00 d6.69 b 180 c+0.80
  t=1.25 d6.48 b   0 c-0.80      t=3.25 d5.52 b  -1 c-0.80
  t=1.50 d5.83 b-180 c+0.80      t=4.00 d3.76 b  -2 c-0.80
  t=2.25 d4.24 b 180 c+0.80      t=4.75 d5.46 b  -1 c-0.80
  Limit cycle: period ~1.75 s, wallDist oscillating 3.76 .. 6.69 m, wheel command
  reversing sign every ~1.5 s, and wallDist FALLING for 0.75-1.00 s of each half cycle
  (5.83 -> 4.24 m and 5.52 -> 3.76 m) while the verb is commanding AWAY at 80%.

DIAGNOSIS: two things compound. (a) SensorBus.ReadWall() reports only the NEAREST of the
four wall planes, so the target identity jumps discontinuously when the robot crosses the
arena mid-line -- the bearing snaps 0 -> 180 and ProgramRunner.RelDrive flips the drive
term from -cos to +cos in one tick, with no hysteresis. (b) F5 (no brake on a timed step)
means the body is still travelling the old way when the sign flips, so the first ~0.9 s of
the new command is spent fighting momentum. RelDrive has no velocity term and no damping.
The R1 AWAY fix holds - it never grinds into a wall - but the verb still does not settle.

--- MAJOR 3: a stalled blocking step burns the FULL 10 s MAX_DUR with zero progress,
    then the hat restarts from the top and stalls again - forever. (This is the deferred
    F8, now quantified.)

PROGRAM (battery G, test drive, wall sensor + compass + 4 wheels, no weapon):
  h0 WALL  WHEN EdgeDist < 1.5  [ MOVE AWAY WALL 85% 1.0s ; TURN BY 90 ]
  h1 IDLE  WHEN always          [ WAIT 0.5 ]

MEASURED - activeStep run-length trace over 18.0 s (h<hat>s<step>:seconds):
  h-1s-1:0.18  h1s0:3.50  h0s-1:0.02  h0s0:1.00  h0s1:10.00  h0s0:1.00  h0s1:2.28
  yaw delta across the whole 10.00 s TURN BY 90 step: 0.0 degrees (held at y=29 exactly).
  wallDist held at 1.0 m, wall bearing held at 147 deg, for 14 s straight.

WHAT THE PROGRAM SAYS: turn 90 degrees. WHAT HAPPENED: 10.00 s of commanded pivot, 0.0
degrees of yaw, then RobotProgram.MAX_DUR expired, the body finished, the hat re-armed
(wall still < 1.5) and ran the identical pair again. Every lower hat is locked out for the
whole time. There is no escalation, no fallback and no give-up.

HONESTY NOTE: the JAM itself was harness-induced - I teleported the robot near a wall and
it tumbled onto its side before settling, so "a 4-wheel box that cannot pivot" is my rig,
not the game. What G measures validly is the RUNNER'S RESPONSE to a stall, and that is
real: StepDone(TurnBy) returns true only on `clock - stepT0 >= MAX_DUR`, and Advance's
body-end path re-arms the same hat in place. The same shape showed up unassisted in the
real fight (battery F): the WALL hat held for 12.22 s continuously - 51% of a 24 s fight -
while wallDist sat at 0.7-1.6 m and the enemy was inside 1.4 m for most of it.

--- MAJOR 4: losing the damage-bus part silently INVERTS every hat that depends on it.
    A robot at 30% hp stops retreating and charges the moment the bus is shot off.

PROGRAM (battery B2):
  h0 ALARM   WHEN HpFrac < 0.5  [ MOVE BACK 70% (SET) ]
  h1 CRUISE  WHEN always        [ MOVE FWD 40% (SET) ]

MEASURED:
  hp forced to 0.300, dmgbus alive:   busValid=True  hat=0  mean wheel cmd = -0.70  (retreat)
  DestroyPart(dmgbus_7), 0.8 s later: busValid=False hat=1  mean wheel cmd = +0.40  (charge)
  hp is still 0.300. Nothing about the robot's condition changed - only its ability to read it.

DIAGNOSIS: ProgramRunner.Read() returns ok=bus.busValid for HpFrac/PowerFrac/PartsLost/
HitRecently, and CondsTrue() treats !ok as FALSE. "I cannot tell" and "I am healthy" are
the same answer. This is the documented sensor-loss semantics (SensorBus: a dead sensor is
"no signal"), and for EnemyRange it is fine. For a SAFETY hat it is exactly backwards: the
one moment the bus dies is the moment you are being taken apart, and the program's response
is to abandon the retreat. The player cannot express "< 50% hp OR I have lost the bus" -
there is no OR and no invalid-sensor condition. Same trap applies to Flipped/TiltUpY if the
tilt sensor is knocked off while the machine is upside down.

--- MINOR 1: one physics step (0.020 s) of ZERO channels on every hat handover that goes
    through Advance's body-end re-arm path.

MEASURED, three independent triggers, all identical:
  D0 (hp trigger, no teleport), per-physics-step mean wheel command:
    [h1 0.40] x8  <SetHp 0.30>  [h0 0.00] [h0 -0.70] [h0 -0.70] ... x17
  B1 (PartsLost trigger): hat flips at 0.020 s, cmd 0.00 on that step, -0.70 after.
  B3 (Flipped trigger):   hat flips at 0.020 s, cmd 0.00 on that step, -0.70 after.
  G gives the smoking gun: the trace contains a segment `h0s-1:0.02` - activeStep is -1
    (no step executed) for exactly one physics step at the handover.
  Real fight (F): 3 of 7 handovers had a zero wheel command on the handover step.

DIAGNOSIS: ProgramRunner.Advance(), body-end branch:
    ReleaseHat();
    if (topNow >= 0) StartHat(topNow);
    return;                 // <-- returns WITHOUT running the new hat's first step
StartHat sets activeStep=-1 and ZeroChannels(), and the `return` skips straight to
ApplyChannels(), which pushes the zeros to the drive. The TriggerEval path does NOT have
this hole (Advance still runs after it in the same FixedUpdate), which is why only some
handovers show it. Cost is 20 ms of coast plus a 20 ms weapon blip - small, but it is the
first step of a preempting SAFETY hat that is thrown away.

--- MINOR 2: WEAPON OFF barely slows a spinner. After 2.0 s of commanded OFF the blade is
    still at 96% speed.

PROGRAM (battery D1): h0 WHEN always [ FOREVER { WEAPON ON ; WAIT 2.0 ; WEAPON OFF ; WAIT 2.0 } ]
MEASURED (actuator.rate, rad/s, 0.2 s samples; F = aiFire true):
  ON  phase: F7.4 F24.6 F34.0 F41.8 F48.4 F53.6 F58.3 F63.1 F67.5 F71.5   (spin-up ~2 s, good)
  OFF phase: -73.1 -72.8 -72.5 -72.2 -71.9 -71.6 -71.3 -71.0 -70.7 -70.4
  Decay = 2.7 rad/s over 2.00 s = 1.35 rad/s per second => ~52 s to reach zero.
  Over the whole 8.8 s trace rate never fell below 70.4.
aiFire toggles at exactly the right instants (step index flips s2 <-> s4 on the 2.0 s
boundaries, to the sample), so the COMMAND is honest; the physical result is not. A player
writing WEAPON OFF to make the machine safe, to stop drawing power, or to set up a wedge
push gets essentially nothing. SPIN_DRAG is the only brake and there is no brake verb.

--- MINOR 3 (latent): FIRE can never complete on a spinner. Actuator.cyclesDone is only
    incremented inside the NON-Spindle branch of Phase.Returning (Actuator.cs:865).
    MEASURED: cyclesDone = 0 after 8.8 s of a spindle running at up to 73.5 rad/s.
    StepDone(Fire) therefore falls through to the `arg * FIRE_CYCLE_TIMEOUT` fallback and a
    FIRE 3 step would block for 12 s doing nothing the program asked for. RobotProgram.
    Validate accepts FIRE on a spinner-only build. LATENT ONLY: POp.Fire is not in
    ProgramCanvas.PLAYER_OPS, so no player can author it today - but old saved programs and
    any harness can.

--- MINOR 4: PowerFrac conditions cannot be exercised in the test bay.
    MEASURED (B4): weapon spun to 73.5 rad/s + 40% drive for 6.0 s in test drive ->
    powerFrac 1.000 -> 0.959 only; combatEnabled=False so PowerPlant ignores the draw.
    A `WHEN power < 50%` hat can never be seen to fire in the bay. In the real fight it
    reached 0.853 in 24 s - still nowhere near the 0.5/0.65 chips the canvas offers.
    Not a defect in the runner; a hole in what the player can test and in what the
    V_FRAC chip list ({0.1 .. 0.9}) implies is reachable.

--- MINOR 5 (test-code only): VerbBench's SIDE TO labels are swapped. VerbBench.cs section E
    calls SideTo(1,...) "LEFT TO" and SideTo(3,...) "RIGHT TO". ProgramCanvas.cs:1156 has
    sides = {FRONT, RIGHT, BACK, LEFT} and ProgramRunner maps idx1 -> +90 deg (target off the
    RIGHT). The GAME is correct and consistent; the bench log lies about which side it tested.

=====================================================================
SUSPECTED BUT UNMEASURED
=====================================================================

1. F6 (a FOREVER whose only blocking step sits behind a false IF) - I did NOT construct it.
   C3b proves the ELSE branch supplies the blocking step, so the C3 family does not hit it.
   Still open, still accepted by Validate.
2. F4 (MOVE TOWARD from a wide bearing) was not re-run this round; I have no independent
   confirmation that the cos-scaling fix still holds. VerbBench section J covers it.
3. A detached weapon: ProgramRunner caches `actuators` at Init from
   GetComponentsInChildren<Actuator>(true). If a weapon part detaches without its
   GameObject being destroyed, the cached reference stays non-null and ApplyChannels would
   keep writing aiFire to a limb that is no longer on the robot. I never got a weapon to
   shed in 50 s of fighting, so this is inference from the code only.
4. Enemy-relative MOVE TO against a moving target closed only 0.21 m per STRIKE entry
   (4 entries, 1.86 s total) in the real fight. That is a weak number but the sample is
   too small and too confounded by preemption to call it a finding.
5. The robot spent 8.78 s (F) and ~19.8 s (E) genuinely flipped (upY measured at -0.23 to
   0.00) with the FLIPPED hat spinning the wheels and never recovering. Whether the game
   intends self-righting to be possible at all is a design question I could not settle;
   what is measurable is that a flipped machine locks its top hat for the rest of the fight.

DEFERRED ITEMS, IMPACT QUANTIFIED (as asked)
=====================================================================
F5 (no brake): in D3 the wheel command reversed at t=1.25 s and the robot kept travelling
  the old way for a further 0.75-1.00 s and 1.6-2.2 m. In the fight, the WALL hat's
  MOVE AWAY 85% for 1.0 s overshot from wallDist 1.2 m to 6.5 m - the arena centre - and
  then fell back to 1.0 m. Every timed step lands roughly twice as far as it reads.
F7 (TURN BY slow): 180 deg of SIDE TO took 3.2 s under program control. In a wall escape a
  TURN BY 90 costs ~2.7 s, which is most of the time the robot has before it is back on
  the wall - see MAJOR 3.
F8 (stall bounds one step): quantified in MAJOR 3 - 10.00 s burned for 0.0 deg, then an
  unbounded repeat. This is the single most damaging deferred item.
